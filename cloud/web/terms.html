Shared groups

You accept these terms when you share a note or join a group. Everyone in a group can read and change all of its notes, and can keep what they downloaded. You are responsible for what you put in a group. Invite only people you know, and accept links only from people you know.

Links to a note

You can share a copy of a note through a link. Anyone who has the link can read that copy until the link ends or you remove it, so send it only to people you mean it for. Everything on this page about what you may not share applies to links too. We cannot read these notes. If someone reports a link and it breaks these terms, we can remove it and tell you.

A list link is a link to a list that stays live. Anyone who has it can tick items and add items, without an account and without accepting these terms, and what they do goes into your note. So choose who gets the link, and end it when it has done its job; you can do that at any time, and the versions of your note keep what the list looked like before. The names people type next to an item are what they typed. Nobody checks them. If something that breaks these terms turns up in a list, we can end the link and tell you, also when someone else put it there.

Reporting and blocking

In a shared group you can report a person or the group, and block a person. After you block someone, you leave the group (or they are removed, if it is your group) and the two of you cannot join a new group together. Other groups you share stay as they are, so leave those yourself if you want to. A block ends when either account is deleted. To undo a block, write to us.

We cannot read the notes in a group. A report reaches us with your email address, the email address of the person you report, and what you wrote. You can also write to hello@jotdrop.net.

What we can do

If someone breaks these terms, we can remove a group or close an account. We tell the people involved by email, unless the law stops us. We also act on valid orders from authorities, but we can only hand over what we have, and that does not include readable notes.

JotDrop Plus

JotDrop Plus is a paid subscription that you buy on our website. The price and how to cancel are shown there before you pay.

Every new account starts with 14 days of JotDrop Plus for free. We don't ask for payment details, and the trial never turns into a paid subscription by itself. We email you 3 days before it ends and on the day it ends. After that your cloud moves to the free plan. Nothing is deleted. What goes beyond the free limits stays, but you can't add more until it fits again or you choose Plus, and note links and list links older than 7 days pause until then. Each account gets the trial once.

New features come to JotDrop Plus first, about 4 weeks before the free plan. With Plus you can also vote on what we build next. We read every vote, but a vote is not a promise that something will be built, or when.

With the family plan, the person who pays can invite up to five others. Each of them gets JotDrop Plus for as long as the family plan is paid, and keeps their own notes and storage. The person who pays can remove someone at any time, and anyone can leave. When the family plan ends, Plus ends for everyone in it.

Mail to JotDrop

With JotDrop Plus you can turn on a secret mail address that turns incoming mail into notes. The address belongs to Plus: when Plus ends, the address stops accepting mail, and it works again when Plus comes back. Mail that already arrived stays yours and still becomes notes.

JotDrop is not a mailbox or an email service. You cannot send or reply from your address, there is no inbox to browse, and we give no guarantee that a given mail arrives. Important mail should not have your JotDrop address as its only destination.

There are limits to keep the service healthy: how large a mail can be, how many can arrive per hour and per day, and how much can wait unconverted. The current limits are shown in the apps and can change; if we lower them, we announce it first. Mail outside the limits is refused, and the sender's own mail server tells them so.

Everything under "What is not allowed" also applies to what you bring in by mail. Using the address to relay spam or abuse to yourself or others can lead to the limits dropping for your address, or to closing the account, as described above.
