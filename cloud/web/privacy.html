JotDrop Cloud

Privacy policy

Last updated: November 14, 2026

Delete your JotDrop account

JotDrop Cloud keeps your JotDrop notes in sync between your devices. This policy covers JotDrop Cloud: this website and the cloud features in the JotDrop plugin for Obsidian, the JotDrop plugin for Joplin, the JotDrop Android app, the JotDrop Web Clipper for Chrome and jotdrop-mcp, the program that connects an AI agent. Using JotDrop without the cloud never needs an account; the Android app policy covers the app itself.

JotDrop is made by Diexar, República Argentina 1 Bajo 7, 03160 Almoradí (Alicante), Spain, which is responsible for your data. Questions or requests: hello@jotdrop.net.

Delete your JotDrop account

This is for JotDrop on Android, from the developer Diexar, and for every other way into JotDrop Cloud. You can do it without the app:

  • On this website: sign in, open Devices & recovery and choose "Delete account".
  • In the Android app: open the JotDrop Cloud screen and choose "Delete account".
  • In Obsidian: open the JotDrop Cloud settings and choose "Delete account".
  • By email: write to hello@jotdrop.net from the address of your account and ask us to delete it. Use this if you can no longer sign in.

Your cloud, account, devices, linked Google or GitHub sign-in and your place in shared groups are deleted at once. A running JotDrop Plus subscription is cancelled and your address is removed from our mailing list. Any backup copies hold the same encrypted data and are replaced over time. Stripe keeps its payment records as the seller. The notes on your own devices stay there.

What we cannot see

Your notes and attachments are encrypted on your device (AES-256-GCM) before they leave it, and file names are replaced by random-looking IDs. Your password never leaves your device. The key that opens your notes stays on your devices and in your recovery code, so neither our server nor we, nor any service named below, can read your notes.

If you connect Joplin desktop with a pairing code, it counts as one of your devices. It asks our server for new notes every 30 seconds, decrypts them on your computer and sends nothing back. Once a note is in Joplin, Joplin's own settings and sync apply to it. Disconnecting Joplin keeps the notes that came in. The Joplin plugin has no account of its own, so delete your account in one of the ways above.

If you connect the JotDrop Web Clipper with a pairing code, it also counts as one of your devices. It encrypts each clip in your browser and sends only the encrypted note; the clipper's own privacy policy explains what it keeps in the browser. "Disconnect this browser" in its settings signs it out when you give its unlock passphrase, and you can always sign it out under Devices & recovery.

If you connect an AI agent with jotdrop-mcp and a pairing code, that program counts as one of your devices too. You run it on a computer or server you choose. It keeps the key to your notes there and decrypts notes in its own memory, so it can hand your agent the notes the agent asks for; the copy it saves on that computer is sealed with your key. It cannot reach your trash or the notes in shared groups. What your agent reads also reaches the AI service behind that agent, under your own agreement with that service. We are not part of that, and our server still sees only encrypted files, as with every other device. Removing it under Devices & recovery stops it at once. What that computer already holds stays readable to whoever has the computer, as with any app, until you run "jotdrop-mcp disconnect" there.

What we store

  • Your email address.
  • What we need to check your sign-in, none of which reveals your password: a salt and key settings, a hash of a login key that your device derives from your password, your note key locked with your password, and, if you turn it on, your authenticator secret encrypted with a server key.
  • If you continue with Google or GitHub: the fixed account ID they give us and your verified email address. Not your name, photo, contacts or repositories.
  • Your devices: the name you give each one, which JotDrop app and version it uses, when it was added and when it last connected.
  • Your encrypted notes and their earlier versions, with their size, the time they were saved and which of your devices saved them.
  • Your plan: free or JotDrop Plus, whether you used the trial, when your trial ends and whether we sent the two emails about it, and for a subscription the Stripe subscription ID, its status and the date it is paid until.
  • If you are in a family plan: which account pays for it, who is in it and when they joined, and the invitations that are not used yet, stored only as a hash.
  • If you vote on what we build next: which ideas you voted for and when. Others see only the total per idea, never who voted.
  • If you turn on Mail to JotDrop: a hash of your mail address, your public mail key, a copy of the address that only your devices can open, your sender filter as hashes, and incoming mail in sealed form until one of your devices turns it into a note. See the Mail to JotDrop section below.
  • If you turn on "Use this key on all my devices" (JotDrop Plus): one copy of your AI key and its settings, sealed by your device with your workspace key, and the time it was saved. See the Ask JotDrop paragraph below.
  • If you tick "Email me news about JotDrop.": when you ticked it and when we added you to our mailing list.
  • For one day after an account is made: a keyed hash of the network (IP) address it was made from, so that one network cannot open many accounts. It is deleted after that day.
  • Short-lived codes, only as keyed hashes. Email codes expire after 15 minutes and Google or GitHub sign-in links after 10 minutes. We count wrong codes per account or email address to stop guessing.

Ask JotDrop is the assistant in the web app. It works with an AI service you pick yourself, with your own key: Anthropic (Claude), OpenAI, Google (Gemini), OpenRouter, or Ollama on your own computer. When you send a request, your browser sends it straight to that service, together with the notes listed under "What goes along" and any note the AI reads while it answers, at most 20 notes or 30,000 characters at a time. You see that list before you send and can take notes off it. Notes in shared groups and notes with a live list link stay out unless you switch them on. "This week" works with a short digest instead. On your device, JotDrop picks the notes that changed in the last 7 days, that have a reminder in the next 7 days, or that have an expiry date within the next 30 days or the last 30 days, at most 40 notes and 30,000 characters. Your browser sends the service only the title, the first line, the open checklist items, the time of the last change, the reminder and the expiry date of each, not the whole note. You see those notes first, you can take any off, and nothing goes until you select Send. "Merge with AI" sends the two notes you chose, in full, and changes a note only after you confirm. "Find duplicates" compares your notes on your device, without AI, and sends nothing. If you choose "Keep both" for a pair, this browser remembers which two notes it was, sealed with your workspace key; that list is not synced and is deleted with your account. A photo, PDF or recording from a note goes along only when you pick it: you tick it in that list, or you choose Transcribe with AI, Read photo with AI or Receipt to Paper drawer on the note, and the file shows by name before you send. Your browser makes a photo smaller first and sends it as a JPEG; a PDF or a recording goes as it is. OpenAI writes out a recording at its own address, with its own transcription model. On OpenRouter, a model that cannot read PDFs gets the text of the PDF instead, which OpenRouter makes with a conversion service it works with. A photo or recording can hold details of other people, so only send what you may share. Text from photo in the editor is different: it reads the photo in your browser and sends nothing to an AI service. None of this passes through our server. Your key stays in this browser, sealed with your workspace key, and is deleted when you log out or turn Ask JotDrop off. It is not synced unless you choose that, as the next paragraph explains. The AI service handles what you send under your own agreement with it; we are not part of that and do not process it for you. Some services may use what you send, files included, to improve their products, Google for example on the free tier of the Gemini API outside the EEA, Switzerland and the UK, so read the terms of the service you pick. With Ollama, what you send stays on your computer. If you dictate a request, your browser's own speech recognition turns your voice into text; in most browsers that is an online service of the browser maker. If you report an answer by email, the email holds the question and the answer, and we use it only to look into the report and reply.

One key on all your devices is part of JotDrop Plus and is off until you turn it on in the settings of Ask JotDrop. When you do, your device seals the AI service, your key, the model and, if you use an address of your own, that address with your workspace key, and sends us only the sealed copy. We keep one copy per account. It is filled up to a standard size, so its length says little about which service you use. We cannot open it. Another of your devices opens it with the same workspace key and offers it to you. That device starts using the key only when you agree, and then keeps its own copy, so Ask JotDrop works without a connection. Right now the web app and the Android app do this; the Obsidian plugin follows. If you turn the switch off, or remove the key on all devices, we delete our copy, and your other devices drop theirs when they next check, which they do about once a day while they are open. If JotDrop Plus ends, nothing is removed, but a new key stays on the device where you set it until Plus is back. A local Ollama model is never shared, and neither is your choice to include notes from shared groups. Whoever gets into your workspace, with your password or your recovery code, can open the sealed copy and use your key. Set a spending limit with your AI service. If you lose a device, make a new key there and save it in JotDrop with the switch on: it replaces the old one on your other devices.

What we do not collect

Your IP address is used in memory to limit repeated attempts; apart from the one-day hash above, it is not written to our database or to logs. JotDrop Cloud has no ads, no analytics and no tracking cookies, and this website loads nothing from other sites, except Cloudflare's person check on the form for a new account. When you use Ask JotDrop, the page also connects to the AI service you chose, and only then. The website keeps you signed in only if you choose "Stay signed in on this browser", and then stores that in your browser.

Services that process data for us

  • Our hosting provider runs our server in Germany. It stores the data above, encrypted notes included.
  • Amazon Web Services (Amazon SES, Frankfurt) sends our emails: sign-in and confirmation codes, security notices and the two emails about your JotDrop Plus trial. It receives your email address and the message.
  • Brevo (France) sends JotDrop news and welcome emails, only if you ticked "Email me news about JotDrop.". It receives your email address. Every email has an unsubscribe link.
  • Stripe handles JotDrop Plus. When you buy Plus we send Stripe your email address and an internal ID of your cloud. Stripe, through Link, sells the subscription to you as the merchant of record and handles your payment details and taxes under its own privacy policy. We never see your card details; we only receive the subscription status.
  • Google and GitHub, only if you choose "Continue with Google" or "Continue with GitHub". They confirm who you are and learn that you signed in to JotDrop Cloud, under their own privacy policies.
  • Cloudflare forwards email you send to hello@jotdrop.net to our mailbox. When you create an account with an email address, Cloudflare Turnstile checks that a person, not a script, does it; for that check Cloudflare sees your IP address and details about your browser.

We do not sell your data or share it with anyone else, unless the law requires it. What you share yourself goes to the people you invite; see below.

Shared groups

You can share notes with people you invite. Everyone in a group can read and change all of its notes. The notes, the group's name and the names people choose are encrypted on your device, so we cannot read them.

Our server does store which accounts are in which group, when something changed, how large the files are and which device saved a version. Other people in the group do not see the device. The owner of a group can see the email addresses of everyone in it, and the join screen says so. Everyone else sees only the name each person chose.

Because we cannot look inside a group, we only learn what is in it if someone tells us. If someone shares something with you that should be reported, use Report in the group or write to hello@jotdrop.net. A report sends us your email address, the email address of the person you report, the group's ID and what you wrote, and we keep that email for as long as we need to handle it. We can then remove the group or the account.

If you block someone, our server stores which account blocked which, so the two of you cannot join a new group together. That stops when either account is deleted.

Links to a note

If you share a note by link, our server stores an encrypted copy of its title and text, which account made the link and when it ends. The key is in the link, in a part that browsers do not send to us, so we cannot read the copy. Anyone with the link can. The link is deleted when it ends, when you remove it or when you delete your account. Someone who reports a link sends us what they write about it, and we see the address of the account that made it.

A list link works the same way, with two differences. First, the copy is a list that anyone with the link can change: tick an item, untick it or add one. Our server keeps the current encrypted version, a version number so that two people ticking at the same time do not undo each other, a hash of a proof that only follows from the key, so that nobody can write without the link, and the time of the last change. Second, people who tick or add an item can type a name. That name goes into the encrypted list, where everyone with the link sees it, and it stays in their own browser so they do not have to type it again. We cannot read it, and nobody checks it. The names stay in the list behind the link; they never go into your note. Your own devices carry the ticks and new items into your note, and your changes into the list, through your account.

Family plan

The person who pays for a family plan sees the email addresses of the people in it. Each of them sees the email address of the person who pays, and the join screen says so. Nobody in a family can read anyone else's notes, and the family plan shares no notes. Someone leaves the family when they leave, are removed or delete their account; the family ends when the person who pays deletes their account.

Mail to JotDrop

With JotDrop Plus you can turn on a secret mail address of your own and email anything to your notes. When a mail arrives, our server reads it once, in memory, to turn it into the text and attachments of a note. It seals everything at that same moment with a key that only your devices hold, and the readable mail is gone. After that, we cannot read the sender, the subject or the content any more, and neither can anyone who gets hold of the server, the database or a backup later. The honest limit: a server that is already compromised at the moment a mail arrives could read that one mail, briefly, like any mail server can.

What stays readable on our side is little: when a mail arrived, how large it is, a hash of your address and, if you use the sender filter, hashes of the addresses you allow. A hash lets the server recognise an address without knowing it. The mail process also keeps a technical log with the IP address of the sending mail server for up to 7 days, to fight abuse. That log never contains the mail itself.

Mail we refuse is not stored at all: when the address does not exist, when JotDrop Plus has ended, when a limit is reached or when the sender is not on your filter, the sending server gets a normal refusal and tells the sender.

A mail usually contains personal data of the person who sent it. You decide what you send or forward to your address; we handle it to carry out our agreement with you. We cannot inform each sender ourselves, because we cannot read who they are; this section is the notice for them too. No new company is involved: mail arrives directly at our own server, and an incoming mail never passes Cloudflare. Your devices later fetch only the sealed copy.

Sealed mail waits until one of your devices turns it into a note and deletes it, or until you delete your account. The note it becomes is an ordinary encrypted note, covered by everything above.

Why we may use it

  • To provide JotDrop Cloud to you (your account, sync, codes and JotDrop Plus, including the 14-day trial and the two emails that tell you when it ends): this is needed for our agreement with you.
  • To send news: your consent, which you can withdraw at any time with the unsubscribe link or by emailing us.
  • To receive mail at your JotDrop address and seal it into notes: this is needed for our agreement with you.
  • To keep one sealed copy of your AI key for your other devices, when you turn that on: this is needed for our agreement with you, because the feature is part of JotDrop Plus.
  • To keep the service safe, for example limiting repeated attempts and keeping the 7-day mail log: our legitimate interest in preventing abuse.
  • To keep payment records: legal obligations, handled by Stripe as the seller.

Transfers outside the EU

Our server runs in Germany, Amazon SES in Frankfurt, and Brevo is based in France. Stripe, Google, GitHub and Cloudflare may process data outside the EU, such as in the United States, under the EU Standard Contractual Clauses or the EU-US Data Privacy Framework.

How long we keep it

  • Your account, devices and encrypted notes: until you delete your account. Earlier versions and deleted notes stay in your cloud, encrypted, until you delete a note forever from the trash. That removes its text and all its versions at once. What stays is a small marker, made of the note's encrypted name, the time and a version number, so your other devices know the note is gone. Files the note used are not removed with it. When a free cloud is full, the oldest versions and deleted notes that only JotDrop Plus can open are removed first; the current version of a note always stays.
  • What you wrote in a shared group stays with the others in that group after you leave or delete your account. We cannot remove what they already downloaded.
  • A group whose owner has left becomes read-only and is deleted after 30 days, unless someone in it takes it over. A group replaced because someone was removed is also read-only and deleted after 30 days.
  • A sign-up that is not confirmed is removed after 15 minutes.
  • Sealed mail: until one of your devices turns it into a note and deletes it, or until you delete your account. The mail log with sender IP addresses: at most 7 days.
  • The sealed copy of your AI key: until you turn it off or remove the key on all devices, or until you delete your account. A backup copy of our server can still hold it for up to 60 days after that.
  • Your votes: until you take them back, the idea leaves the list, or you delete your account.
  • Your place on the mailing list: until you unsubscribe.
  • Email you send to hello@jotdrop.net, a report about an Ask JotDrop answer included: 60 days after the last message in that conversation, unless we need it longer for a dispute or a legal duty.
  • When you delete your account: see Delete your JotDrop account.

Your rights

You can delete your account yourself at any time. You can also ask us for a copy of your data, to correct it, to delete it, to limit or object to its use, or to receive it in a portable form. Your notes are already yours as Markdown files on your own devices. Email hello@jotdrop.net from the address of your account. You can also complain to a data protection authority, such as the Spanish AEPD or the one where you live.
